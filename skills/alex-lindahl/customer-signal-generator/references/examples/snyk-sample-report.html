Executive summary

Snyk sells a developer-first security platform covering code, open-source dependencies, containers, infrastructure as code and APIs, priced per contributing developer and sold to engineering-led software companies. Web

The three strongest signal themes are AI coding volume outrunning security review, security teams being formed or rebuilt, and regulatory clocks led by the EU Cyber Resilience Act, whose reporting duties went live on 11 September 2026. Web

The strongest cluster is the AI Code Surge: an org-wide AI coding assistant rollout, AI agent or MCP work, and an open AppSec hire at the same account. It matches where Snyk is investing (Studio, AI-BOM, Evo) and where bundled competitors are weakest.

High-intent signal clusters

The AI Code Surge

Signals: Org-wide rollout of AI coding assistants (Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code), Building AI agents, LLM apps or MCP servers, Open AppSec, DevSecOps or Product Security role posted in the last 45 days.

Why it matters: The company is producing more code, of a newer kind, than its security team can see, and it has just admitted the gap by hiring for it.

Recommended play:

  1. Target the Head of AppSec or the hiring manager on the open role, plus the Head of AI Platform.
  2. Lead with Snyk Studio: guardrails inside the AI assistant developers already use.
  3. Follow with an AI-BOM scan of a public repo, then an Evo conversation for the agent work.
  4. Reach out within 2 weeks of the posting, before the new hire picks their own stack.

The Compliance Clock

Signals: EU Cyber Resilience Act work (SBOM, PSIRT, vulnerability reporting), Opening an EU entity, office or product line, Compliance programme in flight (PCI DSS, FedRAMP, SOC 2).

Why it matters: A dated obligation (CRA reporting today, SBOMs by December 2027, or an audit) has landed on a company that ships into regulated markets.

Recommended play:

  1. Target the Director of Product Security, with the CISO copied on the second touch.
  2. Lead with the CRA-to-Snyk control map and the 24-hour reporting problem.
  3. Offer an SBOM generation workshop, then a scoped evaluation.
  4. Expect 4 to 8 weeks from signal to first meeting; the deadline sets the pace.

The New Sheriff

Signals: New CISO, Head of AppSec or Head of Product Security in the last 120 days, Open AppSec, DevSecOps or Product Security role posted in the last 45 days, Legacy SAST (Checkmarx, Veracode) named in job postings.

Why it matters: A new security leader is building a team on top of an incumbent tool developers don't use. The stack is up for review.

Recommended play:

  1. Target the new leader directly in their first 60 days.
  2. Lead with a peer benchmark, not a demo.
  3. Offer a side-by-side scan against the incumbent on one repo.
  4. Bring in the VP Engineering once the leader agrees there's a developer-adoption problem.

The Champion Returns

Signals: Former Snyk user joins a non-customer in a security or platform role, Several developers from one company signing up for Snyk Free, Open AppSec, DevSecOps or Product Security role posted in the last 45 days.

Why it matters: A former Snyk user has joined, developers are already trying Snyk Free, and a security role is open. Adoption is pulling from the inside.

Recommended play:

  1. Target the former user first, then the engineering manager behind the sign-ups.
  2. Lead with a fast-start offer that connects existing free accounts into one org.
  3. Route straight to an AE; skip nurture.
  4. Move within 2 weeks; this cluster cools quickly.

Triggers of need

T1. If a company is standing up or rebuilding its application security function, then they likely need a developer-facing security platform, because a small security team cannot review every pull request across hundreds of engineers.

T2. If developers adopt AI coding assistants across the organisation, then they likely need guardrails in the IDE and pipeline (Snyk Studio, Snyk Code), because generated code grows faster than human review capacity.

T3. If a company is shipping AI agents, LLM features or MCP servers, then they likely need AI-BOM and agent-aware testing (Snyk Evo), because prompt injection, tool calls and model supply chains sit outside what classic SAST covers.

T4. If a company sells software or connected products into the EU, then they likely need SCA, SBOM generation and continuous monitoring, because CRA reporting gives 24 hours to flag an exploited vulnerability, which requires knowing what is in every release.

T5. If a company faces a PCI DSS, FedRAMP, SOC 2 or IPO readiness deadline, then they likely need scanning evidence that auditors accept, because auditors expect proof that code and dependencies are tested continuously.

T6. If a company has a public incident or visible dependency exposure, then they likely need fast dependency inventory and fixes, because leadership has urgency and budget to prevent a repeat.

T7. If engineering headcount, services or cloud-native infrastructure are growing quickly, then they likely need container, IaC and per-developer coverage, because each new contributor, image and Terraform module widens the attack surface.

T8. If a company is consolidating security tools or unhappy with an incumbent scanner, then they likely need a platform developers adopt and that covers code to cloud, because tools developers ignore leave findings unfixed, and bundled scanners can leave coverage gaps.

T9. If a Team-plan or free-tier footprint is growing inside an account, then they likely need an Ignite or Enterprise plan, because the Team plan stops at 10 contributing developers.

Competitor displacement

No competitors were specified, so these are inferred from research. Web

GitHub Advanced Security (CodeQL)

Gap: strong detection, but rule customisation is harder and coverage is tied to GitHub. Frustration signal: teams on GitLab or Bitbucket alongside GitHub, or postings asking for multi-SCM coverage. Switching trigger: a second source-control platform or a container and IaC requirement. Message: one platform across every repo and every layer, not only the ones in GitHub.

Wiz Code and other CNAPP bundles

Gap: Wiz Code is newer and shallower than dedicated AppSec tools in developer workflow and code-scanning depth. Frustration signal: security bought the platform, developers aren't using its code findings. Switching trigger: a CNAPP renewal or a developer-adoption review. Message: keep Wiz for cloud posture; use Snyk where developers fix code.

Semgrep

Gap vs Snyk: Semgrep wins on custom rules and fast CI scans, and is the most common Snyk Code replacement. Signal it's being considered: AppSec engineers posting about custom rules or noisy SAST. Message: breadth across SCA, containers and IaC on one developer platform, and automated fixes, rather than rule writing.

Endor Labs

Gap vs Snyk: positioned as reachability-first SCA, a direct challenge to Snyk Open Source. Signal: job posts or talks about 'reachability' and SCA alert fatigue. Message: prioritisation built into a platform that also covers code, containers and AI supply chains.

Checkmarx and Veracode

Gap: mature governance and reporting, but heavier for developers with lower adoption. Signal: legacy SAST named in postings plus a new security leader (the New Sheriff cluster). Switching trigger: renewal or a developer-experience initiative. Message: findings developers act on, measured side by side on one repo.

How to build this

Data architecture

  • First-party: Snyk product usage (contributor counts, free sign-ups by domain), CRM, and marketing engagement. This powers the three highest-scoring signals.
  • Third-party: job postings and people data via Clay search filters, tech stack, funding and news via Clay data points, G2 buyer intent, SEC EDGAR.
  • Enrichment: firmographics and persona contacts via Clay, with work email only for accounts that reach the Active tier.

Signal detection workflow

  1. Ingest: a weekly Clay table of ICP accounts with the free search-filter signals as columns; product signals synced daily from the warehouse.
  2. Score: sum the weights of signals inside their decay window, add 15 when a Section 6 cluster forms, cap at 100.
  3. Enrich: pull persona contacts for accounts scoring 50 or more.
  4. Route: 80+ to the AE this week; 50 to 79 into the matching Section 9 sequence; 25 to 49 to ads and nurture; product signals straight to the account owner.
  5. Trigger: sequences keyed to the signal that fired, so the first line references it.

Recommended stack

  • Detection and monitoring: Clay search filters and scheduled tables; a news monitor for incidents and launches.
  • Enrichment: Clay company and contact data points.
  • Outreach: a sales engagement tool fed by the Clay table.
  • Intent: G2 buyer intent and first-party web analytics.
